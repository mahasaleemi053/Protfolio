Marlow Daily: pop-up to purchase flow

Marlow Daily, a spec probiotic brand, and the four-email Klaviyo welcome flow that follows its 10% off pop-up.

Spec project, not a real brand

The problem

Pop-up discounts collect email addresses, but those subscribers rarely become first-time buyers. Gut-health shoppers are especially wary, having tried both the $50 status bottles and the $10 mystery ones, so a coupon code alone doesn't earn the order. This flow is built to close that gap.

The flow

The flow starts when someone joins the list through the pop-up. The code arrives immediately, a trust email follows on Day 1, an objection-handling email on Day 4, and an expiry reminder on Day 9. The MARLOW10 code is valid for 10 days, so the reminder lands the day before it expires. Each email has one job, and anyone who buys along the way exits the flow so they never get reminders for something they already ordered.

Why it's built this way

Meeting the CFU objection early

Shoppers walk past 50 and 100 billion CFU bottles and assume bigger numbers mean a better product. Left unanswered, that doubt turns into an abandoned cart, or into someone quitting in week one after a high-dose bottle upsets their stomach. Email 3 addresses it head-on, explaining the die-off reaction in plain language and positioning 15 billion CFU across 6 strains as a calibrated daily dose.

Holding the rational middle

Marlow Daily is priced at $29 on subscription, between $10 to $15 budget bottles and $50 to $60 premium ones. Email 2 makes that gap the story, so the 10% off code reads as a fair introduction to a fairly priced product, not a sign of a cheap one. The reminder in Email 4 is a plain deadline with a quick recap, in keeping with the honest tone of the rest of the sequence.
